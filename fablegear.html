FABLEGEAR
Library repair for rekordbox · macOS · Open beta

Your library is worth more than the software managing it.

FableGear is an open source macOS app that fixes the problems a rekordbox library develops over years: tracks it can no longer find, the same song filed four times, and tempo and key values you can't fully trust. It runs on your own machine, reads your real library, and shows you what it found before it changes anything.

It is in open beta, and the beta is free. We are still deciding how the finished thing should behave — which is the entire reason for the survey below.

Why we built it

Rekordbox tracks your music by storing the exact location of every file. That works until a file moves. Rename a drive, reorganize folders, or restore from a backup that missed something, and tracks that played fine last week show up as missing when you load a set.

The database rekordbox keeps is a closed file. When the references inside it break, there is no screen that tells you which ones broke or why, and most advice online assumes you still have a healthy library. That is not much use once yours already has three hundred missing tracks in it.

We also got tired of paying four separate companies to do four things to the same folder of music, with no single place to see the results. So FableGear reads the file references your library actually uses, repairs the ones pointing nowhere, and reports what it found. Your cue points, hot cues, playlists, and ratings are not touched.

What you can do with it

  • [ 01 ] REPAIR Reconnect tracks rekordbox lost When a drive gets renamed or a folder moves, FableGear finds the files rekordbox can no longer locate and points the library back at them. Cue points, hot cues, ratings, and playlist membership stay as they were.
  • [ 02 ] DEDUPLICATE Find the same song filed more than once It compares audio fingerprints rather than filenames, so it recognizes a raw rip and the same track after a Mixed In Key or Platinum Notes pass as one song. Duration is part of the match, which is what stops a 3:30 radio edit from being merged with a 7:00 extended mix. You choose what to keep, and playlist membership moves to the copy you keep instead of being lost.
  • [ 03 ] ANALYZE Tempo and key you can check FableGear detects BPM and musical key and writes them to your file tags. It also reports how confident it was, so you can review the handful of tracks it was unsure about instead of trusting all of them equally.
  • [ 04 ] RECOVER Rebuild playlists from a USB stick If your laptop library is gone but you still have an exported drive, FableGear can read the crates off that drive and rebuild them, then write them back into a live rekordbox library.
  • [ 05 ] ORGANIZE Rename and sort by what you choose Rename files from their tags and sort them into folders by label, artist, genre, or the structure of your own crates. Every move is written to a log first, so you can see what happened and undo it.
  • [ 06 ] EXPORT Write a USB that a CDJ reads FableGear can write the Pioneer library format and the analysis files that go with it, including beat grids and waveforms. See the honest note about this below.

Numbers, and how we got them

The benchmark methodology and the 91.4% exact-match number behind the tempo-detection claim.

Claims about analysis quality are easy to make and hard to check, so here is ours with the method attached. Rekordbox writes its own beat grid for every track it analyzes. We read 12,687 of those grids out of one working DJ library and used them as the answer key, then ran a random 300-track sample through FableGear and compared.

Beat detectionWithin 4%Within 1%Exact
Our previous method90.7%36.8%13.4%
What ships now98.3%94.8%91.4%

The last column is the one that matters. A tempo that is 4% off drifts a full beat within about 25 bars, which is useless for a beat grid even though it looks fine as a percentage. Exact agreement with rekordbox on 91.4% of tracks is what makes a grid worth putting on a USB stick.

We also found and fixed a real bug in our own loudness tool while measuring this. It was raising volume to hit a target without checking how close the audio already was to maximum, which clipped the file and deleted the original. On the tracks we tested it damaged 39 out of 39. It now refuses to raise volume past the point where clipping starts, and tells you when it cannot reach the target instead of ruining the file to get there.

What that measurement does not prove: it is one library, with one person's taste in music, and 300 tracks. A collection of drum and bass or hip hop would score differently. We publish the method so you can disagree with it.

How it compares to DJ software

Where FableGear overlaps with Mixed In Key, Platinum Notes, Lexicon, and Rekord Cloud — and where it doesn't try to.

FableGear does not try to beat the specialists at their own specialty. It does the library maintenance parts in one free local app instead of four paid ones.

  • REKORDBOXStill required for some thingsRekordbox is a player and a performance tool, and it is good at that. It is not built to keep a large archive readable through years of drive changes. FableGear does the archive work and leaves performance to rekordbox. You can run both.
  • MIXED IN KEYKey and energy analysisMixed In Key is a mature paid product and its energy ratings have no equivalent here. Our key detection is good but we have not measured it against an answer key the way we did with tempo, so we are not going to claim it is better. FableGear is free and includes key detection as part of library work rather than as a separate purchase.
  • PLATINUM NOTESLoudness and correctionPlatinum Notes is a paid batch processor that rewrites your audio. FableGear measures loudness to the same broadcast standard on your own machine for free, but it is deliberately more conservative: it will decline to change a file rather than clip it. If you want aggressive per-track mastering, Platinum Notes does that and we do not.
  • LEXICONLibrary management across appsLexicon is a polished paid app that syncs libraries between rekordbox, Serato, Traktor, and others. It is more finished than FableGear and supports more platforms. FableGear is free, open source, macOS only, and focused on rekordbox specifically, including reading and writing the Pioneer USB format directly.
  • REKORDCLOUDCloud library toolsRekord Cloud does library conversion and cleanup through a browser, on a subscription. FableGear does its work on your machine with no account and no upload. If you prefer a hosted service with support, that is a fair reason to pick theirs.

How it compares to general file tools

Against Picard, Mp3tag, dupeGuru, and Finder itself — and why none of them know what a cue point is.

Plenty of people already clean music folders with tools that were not written for DJs. Those tools are often excellent, and they do not know what a cue point is.

  • MUSICBRAINZ PICARDFingerprint taggingPicard is free, cross platform, and better than FableGear at filling in correct release metadata from MusicBrainz. It also happily reorganizes files in ways that leave rekordbox unable to find them. FableGear uses the same fingerprinting service but updates the rekordbox database alongside the files, so tagging does not break your library.
  • MP3TAGBulk tag editingMp3tag is faster and more flexible for pure tag editing, and it has years of refinement behind it. It has no idea your library exists. FableGear edits fewer fields but keeps the database in agreement with what it wrote.
  • DUPEGURU / GEMINIDuplicate findersGeneral duplicate finders match on filename, size, or hash. That misses the case DJs actually have, where the same song exists as a raw rip and again after processing, so the bytes differ completely. FableGear matches on audio fingerprint and duration, and shows you which playlists each copy is in before you delete anything.
  • FINDER, FOOBAR2000, VLCMoving files and auditioning themMoving files in Finder is exactly what breaks a rekordbox library, because Finder does not update the database. FableGear moves files and updates the references in the same operation. It also has preview decks built in, so you do not need a second player open to hear what you are about to change.

What it is built on

The open source dependencies FableGear runs on, and their licenses.

FableGear is Python. It leans on open source projects that are better at their jobs than anything we would write, and it is worth knowing what they are, because they determine what the app can and cannot do. Our own code is MIT licensed. These are installed from the Python package index on your machine when you first run the app rather than shipped inside the download.

Audio analysis

ffmpegLGPL / GPLDecodes every format, measures loudness and true peak to the EBU R128 standard, and handles format conversion. Installed separately with Homebrew. Nearly everything audio in FableGear goes through it.
essentiaAGPL-3.0Beat tracking, from the Music Technology Group at Universitat Pompeu Fabra. This is the single biggest reason our tempo numbers improved from 13% to 91% exact. It is optional: if there is no build available for your macOS and Python version, the app installs without it, falls back to a less accurate method, and says so in its health panel rather than quietly giving you worse grids.
librosaISCMusical key detection, waveform generation, and the tempo fallback when essentia is unavailable. Works everywhere, which is why it stays.
pyacoustid and ChromaprintMIT / LGPLAudio fingerprinting, used to recognize duplicates and to look up metadata from AcoustID and MusicBrainz. This is what lets duplicate detection work on processed copies.

Reading and writing your library

pyrekordboxMITReads and writes the rekordbox database and its analysis files. Dylan Jones' work here is the reason an app like this is possible at all.
mutagenGPL-2.0-or-laterReads and writes tags across MP3, AIFF, FLAC, WAV, and M4A. Every tag FableGear touches goes through it.
numpy and soundfileBSDNumerical work and audio file inspection, including reading the bit depth of a source file so a conversion does not silently downgrade it.

The app itself

Flask, Waitress, pywebviewBSD / ZPLFableGear runs a small web server on your machine and shows it in a native window. That is why the interface looks like a web app: it is one, served only to you, on localhost.
yt-dlpUnlicensePowers the download tool. What you do with it is your responsibility.
Model Context Protocol SDKMITLets an AI assistant drive FableGear's tools directly, with a required order so it cannot deduplicate a library it has not audited yet.

Two projects deserve credit without being dependencies. The reverse engineering of Pioneer's USB formats by Deep Symmetry (crate-digger and dysentery) and by Jan Holthuis (rekordcrate) is what made the export work possible. We did not figure that format out; we read their documentation.

What we are not going to pretend about

The USB export works, once, on one player. We wrote a USB stick with FableGear, with no rekordbox involved, and it loaded and played on a CDJ-3000. That is a real result and it changes what this app can become. It is also one player on one day. We have not tested an XDJ, an OPUS-QUAD, or older 2000NXS2 hardware, and until we do, take a rekordbox-made stick to the gig as well.

Loudness normalization will often decline to do anything. Most commercial music is already mastered close to maximum. Our default target asks for more volume than those files have room for, so the tool correctly skips them instead of clipping. That is the safe behavior, but it means the feature does less than the name suggests. We would rather it do nothing than damage your audio.

It is macOS only. There is no Windows build. The code is not deeply tied to macOS, but nobody has done the work, so we are not going to promise a date.

It is young. There are 528 automated tests and the destructive operations are the most heavily tested parts, because those are the ones that can cost you something. It is still a young project maintained by a small team. Back up your library before you point any new tool at it, including this one.

It writes to your files and your database. That is the entire point, and it is also the risk. Every operation that changes something writes to a log first and can be undone, and destructive tools default to a preview run. Read the preview.

Where this is going

Right now FableGear supports rekordbox and expects you to keep using it. That support is not going away. Everything the app does is designed to leave your rekordbox library working.

The longer aim is that you should not need rekordbox to get music onto a player. The USB export result above is the first evidence that is achievable rather than wishful. If it holds up across more hardware, the choice of what manages your collection stops being made for you by whoever built the gear. That is the reason the source is public: a library tool you depend on should not be something that can be taken away from you.

What it will cost, and what it won't

We would rather tell you this now, while you are deciding whether to install a beta, than spring it on you later.

The beta is free, and it stays free. Everything you can download today — every version numbered 1.x — costs nothing and always will.

The release build is what changes. It ships when three things are true: the app is code-signed and notarized so Gatekeeper stops fighting you, the USB export is verified on more than one player, and enough of you have told us what is still broken. At that point it becomes a one-time purchase in the $9.99–$16 range. One payment. No subscription, not now and not later. There is also a pay-what-you-want option above that floor, for anyone who wants to fund the hardware testing that makes the rest possible.

We are not putting a date or a version number on that, because we do not have one. It happens when the feedback says it should.

The source stays open It is MIT licensed and it stays that way. Anyone who wants to build it themselves can, for nothing, forever. What you would be buying is the signed, notarized, self-updating build — the convenience, not the capability.
Everyone in the beta is grandfathered If you are running a 1.x build before the release ships, you keep it. Free, permanently, including the paid release and every update after it. Leave an email at the end of the survey and that is the list we will use.
Nothing gets taken away No feature that works in the beta will be moved behind the purchase later. The free build will always tell you exactly what is wrong with your library.
Being straight about the change: an earlier version of this page said the app would be free, full stop. That was written before we understood what verifying this on real hardware costs. We would rather change the plan in the open, with the reasoning attached, than quietly ship a price later — so if the sentence above is a dealbreaker, the source is still public and that has not changed.
Why we're asking

Three people's blind spots aren't the DJ community's

FableGear was built to fix problems in our own library, then a shared one. That's a couple of working DJs and a lot of hours in one setup — not a representative sample of how everyone actually works. Before this goes wider, we want to know what a library setup outside our bubble looks like, and where FableGear's assumptions don't hold.

We are asking about everything that touches your music, not just DJ software: the player you audition in, the tool that tags it, the thing you trust to back it up, and where you buy it. The parts that break tend to be the seams between those, and seams are exactly what we can't see from inside one setup.

What this section won't do: there's no mailing list you're forced onto, no dark patterns, and no requirement to have installed FableGear yet. The download is not behind this survey — it's right down there whenever you want it. We're asking because the answers change what gets built, not to make you pay a toll.
